Child Safety Standards

Standards against child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE)

Published by: Atlanta Ayyappa Seva Sangam (AAYSSA)
Effective date: November 10, 2026

AAYSSA has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation, child sexual abuse material (CSAM), grooming, sextortion, trafficking, or any behavior that sexualizes, exploits, or endangers a child.

Scope

These standards apply to the AAYSSA membership portal, public forms, community programs, communications, user-submitted content, volunteers, administrators, and any activity organized or facilitated through AAYSSA services. A child is anyone under 18 years of age.

Prohibited conduct and content

Users must not create, upload, request, share, link to, promote, or facilitate:

  • CSAM or any sexual depiction or sexualization of a child, including computer-generated or altered material
  • Sexual solicitation, grooming, coercion, sextortion, or attempts to arrange sexual contact with a child
  • Sexual trafficking, exploitation, or commercial sexual activity involving a child
  • Instructions, encouragement, normalization, or communities supporting CSAE
  • Sharing a child's intimate or identifying information for exploitative purposes
  • Any other content or conduct that violates applicable child-safety laws

Reporting a concern

If a child is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services first.

Report suspected CSAE, unsafe conduct, or content involving a child to AAYSSA at aayssa.official@gmail.com. Include only the information needed to identify the concern. Do not download, copy, forward, or attach suspected CSAM.

Open AAYSSA Reporting Form

Reports may also be made to the appropriate national or local child-safety reporting authority. In the United States, suspected online child sexual exploitation may be reported to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org.

Review and enforcement

AAYSSA reviews child-safety reports promptly and limits access to reports to people responsible for safety and response. Depending on the circumstances, AAYSSA may:

  • Remove or restrict content and preserve relevant records securely
  • Suspend or terminate portal access and participation in AAYSSA activities
  • Restrict a person from contacting children or participating in youth activities
  • Refer a matter to law enforcement, child-protection services, NCMEC, or another appropriate authority
  • Cooperate with lawful investigations and mandatory reporting requirements

AAYSSA does not notify an alleged offender when doing so could increase risk to a child or interfere with an investigation.

Child-safety contact

AAYSSA's designated contact for questions about these standards and reports concerning the portal is aayssa.official@gmail.com.

Privacy and record handling

Child-safety information is handled according to the AAYSSA Privacy Policy and applicable law. AAYSSA may retain reports and related records as needed for safety, legal compliance, investigations, and prevention of repeat abuse.

Updates

AAYSSA may update these standards as its services, best practices, or legal responsibilities change. The effective date above identifies the current published version.
